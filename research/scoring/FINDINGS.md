## Findings that change the next phase

### Review needs several routes, and our current route needs revision

The November rulebook t.174 names the Head Referee as the authority to settle an
appeal. The August 2026 Operations Manual, PDF p.15, describes the Head Referee
deciding whether an on-strip protest is reviewable, and the full Bout Committee
convening for a reviewable protest. It also ends that opportunity once the fencer
unhooks or fences another touch. PDF p.16 separately discusses Jury of Appeal
review of non-appellate Bout Committee decisions. These roles are more nuanced
than the app's universal Head Referee statement.

The official manual directory says to defer to the current Athlete Handbook.
The handbook pp.82–83 has a separate recorded-score correction procedure; that
procedure is not an appeal of observed touch validity. Rulebook t.56 equipment
annulment, t.60–62 official video and t.172–175 rule-application protest have
different triggers and timing. Referee signals notes also allow a polite request
for fuller phrase analysis (t.63): explanation is distinct from formal appeal.

**Unresolved:** reconcile the precise domestic on-strip authority/scope and
timing across t.174 and Operations Manual p.15. Both passages have been visually
checked in the PDFs. Do not claim the old one-route wording is comprehensive.
The common facts/rule distinction and named eligible requesters agree. Before
expanding procedural content, revise the guide to acknowledge the domestic
procedure and, if needed, ask the event's officials/Referees' Commission for a
specific clarification. No messages to officials have been sent.

### “Both lights” needs score and phase context

Ordinary valid épée doubles are only one case. Pool 4–4, deciding extra minutes,
tied-time extra minutes and team relay targets have separate clauses; regulation DE finishing doubles remain a narrower unresolved question. t.41.1 even permits
an extra penalty hit to take a relay beyond the usual increment. We should ask
for format/score/phase when those conditions affect an explanation, without
requiring them to find a general entry. Do not silently extrapolate a 15-touch
outcome into every youth/veteran variant.

### Timing at Halt differs from timing at the buzzer

t.23 allows an already-started movement after Halt in appropriate circumstances.
t.44 expiration-of-time rules have a distinct cutoff, including the unlinked
timekeeper stop. This is a high-value missing entry: our current Halt summary
must not be applied universally to time expiration.

Sources: [t.23](../articles/usa-rules-2025-11/t.23.txt),
[t.44](../articles/usa-rules-2025-11/t.44.txt).

### Equipment rules need to remain weapon-specific

Shared preservation and testing requirements live in t.56. The annulment lists
are foil t.80–81, épée t.93–95, sabre t.99; the exceptions differ. Candidate
entries include broken blades, intermittent faults, spurious lights, a light
suppressed by a subsequent hit, oxidation/paint/loose-tip exceptions, and sabre
insulation that prevents annulment. A fault establishes material possibility,
not proof of an unregistered historical point.

### Local-strip assumptions require event context

Operations Manual p.17 permits smaller local/division qualifier strips and says
grounded strips are preferred but not required. Rulebook t.18 explicitly points
to domestic event-level strip requirements. Conductive runoff is not legal
combat area simply because the ground circuit extends there. Our floor case
must not silently assume every event has an FIE-standard grounded strip.

Sources: [Operations p.17](../sources/usa-fencing/2026-27-operations-manual.pdf#page=17),
[t.18](../articles/usa-rules-2025-11/t.18.txt),
[m.57](../articles/usa-rules-2025-11/m.57.txt).

### Apparatus timing is not a priority rule

USA Appendix B specifies an épée two-hit tolerance band: under 40 ms must
register both, over 50 ms must register only the first. Foil's 300 ±25 ms blocking
and sabre's 170 ±10 ms interval are different, and foil explicitly says apparatus
timing is unrelated to the convention's fencing time. Avoid a single exact
“simultaneous” number or suggesting the box decides priority.

Sources: [USA Appendix B](../articles/usa-rules-2025-11/appendix-b.txt),
[FIE Material Rules](../sources/fie/2026-08-material.pdf) Appendix B for comparison.

### Official guidance helps where a short clause is insufficient

The February 2025 domestic sabre guard FAQ distinguishes incidental follow-
through contact to the mask, actual scoring by the guard, dangerous/forceful
blows, who caused the contact, and contact elsewhere on the body. It explicitly
limits this interpretation to sabre. This is a useful pattern for future entries:
small variants with their facts, rather than a broad synonym leading to one
unconditional outcome. The handbook p.39 also identifies conductive foil/sabre
bibs as inappropriate in épée because they may ground the opponent's tip.

Sources: [official FAQ capture](../extracted/usa-referee-faq.txt),
[announcement](../extracted/usa-bell-guard-2025-02-12.txt),
[Handbook p.39](../sources/usa-fencing/2026-27-athlete-handbook-2026-09-27.pdf#page=39).

### Private footage and official video are different sources of evidence

The handbook's scoresheet procedure explicitly rejects fencer/spectator video
and photos as score documents; that statement must retain its scoresheet scope.
Official video review has its own requester and allocation rules in t.60–62.
Operations Manual p.19 makes video optional at local/regional events. Do not
apply FIE o.105 international mandatory schedules to every USA tournament, or
extend the handbook statement into an unsupported universal ban on all footage.

Sources: [Handbook p.82](../sources/usa-fencing/2026-27-athlete-handbook-2026-09-27.pdf#page=82),

1. Reconcile procedural wording first; split explanation, equipment test, official
   video, rule application and recorded score into distinct explanations.
2. Extend épée with final-score/overtime doubles, rear exits, one/both feet off,
   falls, broken blades and the remaining apparatus fault exceptions.
3. Add clock expiration, restart position, team score carryover and verified
   domestic age formats as shared context.
4. Add foil/sabre equipment, target exceptions and the official sabre guard cases.
5. Expand priority only with carefully bounded examples and current referee
   interpretation. Written rules are indexed; disputed visual classification is
   not solved by extracting them.

Search candidates in `topics.json` are draft retrieval vocabulary, not additions
to the live app. A later content pass should add reviewed phrases and positive,
negative and cross-weapon tests with each new case.

## Deliberate limits and unresolved leads

- Source extraction is complete for the collected rulebooks, but every future
  scenario is not yet independently reviewed or ready for publication.
- Priority convention text has been read and indexed. The official referee
  development channel is linked in the resources capture, but its videos have
  not been downloaded/transcribed or treated as binding rule changes. Seminar
  agendas indicate topics, not rulings. Current borderline priority practice is
  a specific follow-up, not a reason to invent answers now.
- Operations/handbook contain older-numbered cross-references (for example the
  handbook's “t.105” exclusion passage and its old format citation numbers).
  Preserve those publisher documents; map by subject to current articles rather
  than silently editing the source or trusting every inherited number.
- The handbook's Y8 developmental format (pp.84–85), local formats (pp.83–84),
  table of national formats (pp.37–38) and age-specific breaks need careful scope.
  Do not publish a universal Y8/10 format from one paragraph.
- Parafencing, NCAA, international-event qualifications, ratings/selection
  points, commercial equipment certification and medical advice are outside
  this companion's standing USA bout-scoring inventory. Their source material
  remains archived; those rule systems need explicit separate scope.
- October t.124/t.72/t.20 updates remain the completed shared audit. They have not
  been re-derived or replaced with the historical November wording in this phase.

The library and inventory provide a stable starting point for these follow-ups.
New research should target a named gap, changed document or real scenario.

## Implementation reconciliation — September 30, 2026

The companion now distinguishes explanation, equipment testing, official video,
rule-application protest and scoresheet correction. It cites the domestic manual’s
Head Referee screening / full Bout Committee review and stay-hooked timing beside
t.174’s older wording. Event officials identify the applicable route; the guide
does not silently claim the two texts say the same thing. The handbook supplies
the separate scoresheet route. This resolves the publication gap by exposing scope
and source wording, without inventing precedence or contacting officials.

The legacy t.117 collision is repaired and regression-tested against the real
article. A research citation to o.79 for finishing doubles was incorrect: that
article concerns withdrawal. It has been removed. Pool 4–4 and extra-minute rules
are supported; the collected texts do not explicitly settle regulation DE 14–14
doubles. That narrower question remains unpublished pending authoritative guidance.

The first implementation expansion publishes 33 bounded situations with 73 verified excerpts. Research topic statuses identify implemented portions. Remaining priority, adapted-format and regulation-DE finishing-double questions are not implied coverage.
